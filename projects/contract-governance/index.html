The business question

Contract dates, owners, obligations, and renewal decisions were difficult to review as one portfolio. Late visibility compressed negotiation time and increased the risk of rushed extensions or missed commercial actions.

The goal was to make the next decision clearer, not simply produce another report.

How I approached it

I started by consolidating the information needed to make a renewal decision: agreement dates, owners, value, obligations, commercial status, and the time required for review. When these details were viewed together, it became clear that the main problem was not the expiration date itself; it was how late the organization gained visibility to the decision.

I grouped agreements by risk and introduced lead-time triggers so complex or high-value renewals entered review earlier. The future workflow connected stakeholder intake, commercial review, negotiation, approval, and signature, with a clear escalation path when an owner or decision was delayed. Regular portfolio reviews then focused on upcoming decisions and blockers rather than searching for basic contract information.

What I delivered

The governance model combined a portfolio view for more than thirty agreements, a renewal calendar, risk and escalation criteria, and a standard owner-and-next-action review. This gave stakeholders a visible pipeline and protected the time needed for a deliberate negotiation or sourcing decision before expiration pressure took control.

Business impact

I managed the supplier lifecycle for 12+ indirect vendors in Agiloft CLM. Standardized templates reduced contract execution time by 50%.

What I learned

Contract governance is most valuable before a deadline becomes urgent. Earlier visibility protects negotiation leverage and gives stakeholders time to make a deliberate decision.
