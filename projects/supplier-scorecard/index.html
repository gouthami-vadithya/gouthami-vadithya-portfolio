The business question

Supplier discussions were reactive and spread across delivery updates, expedites, quality issues, and cost conversations. Teams needed one shared view of performance, risk, ownership, and due dates.

The goal was to make the next decision clearer, not simply produce another report.

How I approached it

I first looked at how supplier issues were being discussed each week. Delivery delays, expedite requests, quality concerns, and unanswered follow-ups appeared in different conversations, which made it difficult to see whether a supplier had an isolated miss or a recurring performance problem.

I brought those signals together by defining measures for delivery, quality, lead time, OTIF, expedite activity, and response time. I then organized the scorecard around trends and thresholds rather than a single weekly snapshot. During supplier reviews, the data was used to focus the discussion on the few issues that created the most operational risk. Each discussion ended with a named owner, a corrective action, and a due date so the following review could begin with accountability instead of another status recap.

What I delivered

The scorecard established a weekly operating rhythm supported by performance trends, corrective-action tracking, escalation criteria, and a consistent supplier-review agenda. Procurement and stakeholders could now enter the same conversation with a shared fact base and see whether earlier actions had actually improved performance.

Business impact

The scorecard created a common fact base for supplier conversations and supported stronger accountability. It helped the team sustain 98% delivery performance while focusing attention on recurring risks.
