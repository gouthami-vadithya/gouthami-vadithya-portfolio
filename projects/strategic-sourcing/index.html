The business question

Complex sourcing decisions combined price, commercial terms, lead time, capability, and risk. Without a consistent evaluation model, stakeholder preferences could outweigh evidence and the lowest bid could appear better than the best business outcome.

The goal was to make the next decision clearer, not simply produce another report.

How I approached it

I began each sourcing event by working with stakeholders to turn a broad request into requirements that suppliers could answer consistently. That early definition mattered because a low quotation could look attractive while excluding implementation needs, lead-time risk, commercial assumptions, or ongoing costs.

Once proposals arrived, I normalized the bids and built a total evaluated cost view that made those differences comparable. Supplier questions and stakeholder feedback were brought back into the model rather than handled as side conversations. I used the resulting fact base to prepare negotiations, test alternatives, and explain the tradeoffs behind the recommendation. This allowed the award discussion to focus on total value and execution confidence instead of simply defending the lowest price.

What I delivered

Across more than ten RFx events, I created structured bid comparisons, total-cost models, supplier evaluation scorecards, negotiation fact bases, and documented award recommendations. The materials showed not only which supplier ranked highest, but also what assumptions drove the decision and what risks still needed to be managed after award.

Business impact

Across more than $20M of addressed spend, the sourcing work produced $540K in verified savings and a selected solution with 9% lower total evaluated cost.
