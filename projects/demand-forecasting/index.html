The planning question

A seasonal product family showed changing demand, promotional spikes, and forecast bias. The goal was not simply to calculate a forecast. It was to determine which method gave the business the most useful signal and then connect that signal to supply decisions.

A forecast becomes valuable only when it changes a planning decision.

How I approached it

I began by cleaning the demand history and separating recurring seasonality from promotional spikes and unusual periods. I then built moving-average, exponential-smoothing, and seasonal alternatives and compared them using MAPE, bias, and the pattern of forecast error. The preferred model was not simply the most complex one; it was the method that produced a stable, understandable signal for planning.

After selecting the forecast, I converted it into base, upside, and downside demand scenarios. Each scenario was linked to a different inventory and capacity response, then placed inside a monthly demand review, supply review, gap discussion, and executive decision cycle. This connected the statistical work to the moment when the business would decide whether to hold the plan or change it.

What I built

The academic case produced a forecast-comparison workbook, seasonal demand profile, exception log, three planning scenarios, and an S&OP calendar with owners and decisions. The executive summary explained how a change in demand should alter inventory or capacity, making the forecast useful beyond the accuracy calculation itself.

Modeled impact

The recommended process reduced forecast error in the modeled scenario and gave planners a clearer trigger for when to adjust inventory or capacity. Results are academic and scenario-based, not employer outcomes.

Decision-ready forecast

The project connected statistical accuracy to practical choices about stock, capacity, and service risk.

What I learned

Forecast accuracy matters, but planning discipline matters just as much. A repeatable review cycle helps the organization react to new information without constantly rewriting the plan.
