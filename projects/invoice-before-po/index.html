The business question

Leaders could see individual exceptions, but not the pattern behind them. PO, invoice, contract, supplier, and cost-center records lived in separate views, so recurring issues were difficult to isolate and assign.

The goal was to make the next decision clearer, not simply produce another report.

How I approached it

I began by tracing how an exception appeared from the moment an invoice arrived through the point when a reviewer had to explain it. That exercise showed that confirming orders, missing contracts, expired agreements, and invoice-date mismatches were being discussed as separate problems even when they came from the same purchasing behavior.

I translated those patterns into consistent business rules, then connected PO, invoice, supplier, contract, and cost-center data in one reporting model. Instead of designing a dashboard around the available fields, I designed it around the questions leaders were asking: how large is the exposure, where is it concentrated, why did it happen, and who needs to act? The final view moved from an executive summary into supplier and department-level detail, with reason categories that made follow-up more specific.

What I delivered

The finished solution combined an executive KPI view, supplier and cost-center drilldowns, and a practical exception taxonomy. I also structured the review process so each issue could be assigned to an owner and carried into the next discussion with a clear action. The dashboard therefore became more than a compliance count; it provided a repeatable way to investigate patterns and address their causes.

Business impact

The work created a common fact base for reviewing compliance across more than $20M in purchasing activity. It made recurring exceptions visible by supplier, cost center, and cause without claiming savings that the dashboard itself did not generate.
